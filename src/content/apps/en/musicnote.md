The problem is rarely that you cannot read music. It is that every note starts with counting up from do, and counting never turns into reading no matter how long you do it. Closing that gap is what this app is for. One note appears on the staff, you tap one of do·re·mi·fa·sol·la·si, and that is the whole loop.

## Decide what can come up

Practice goes stale when it starts too wide. Treble and bass clef each turn on and off on their own, and each one you turn on gets its own range.

- **Easy · Hard · Very hard** — up to 1, 3, and 5 ledger lines. The ranges stack, so picking Hard keeps the easy notes in the mix.
- The range you pick is drawn on a staff right there, with a count of how many notes it holds, so you know how wide the session is before it starts.
- Turn a clef off and its range is remembered, then restored when you turn it back on. You are not re-picking it every time.

## Decide how they show up

The same range trains something different depending on what you are after, so the style is two axes multiplied together.

- **How it shows** — plain, or count from do. Counting labels the nearest do and marks the path from there to the note with a dotted outline. It is the stage where you build the counting habit instead of memorising a position you cannot read yet.
- **Which notes** — all, lines only, spaces only, or do·re·si. Drill lines alone if lines and spaces blur together; drill do·re·si alone if you want the landmarks first.
- Counting from do adds a direction: up, down, or either. If one direction is reliably slower for you, that is the one you work.
- The whole card is pinned just above Start, and tapping its title folds it down to a single line naming the combination you chose.

## There is no finish line

A session is not a fixed number of questions. Notes keep coming until you tap Exit in the top left. Reading with a countdown left in the corner is a different exercise from simply reading.

- A wrong answer never spells the right one out. The note stays on the staff and you read it again. Copying an answer down and moving on is how you miss it the same way next time.
- Each question carries its own clock. Retries after a wrong answer keep that clock running, and only first-try answers count toward your average.
- No progress bar, no remaining count. The screen holds the note you are reading and the answer buttons.

## Each session becomes one line

Exit drops you back on Stats, where a session is one row.

- Date and time, the types you picked, and how many of the questions you got on the first try and how long it took — all on the one row.
- Tap the row and the session opens up: a summary plus a per-question breakdown, so you can see which note ate the time.
- Sessions you quit part way through stay in the list but are left out of the totals. A day you cut short after five minutes does not drag your average around.

## Go back over the weak ones

Start studying, at the bottom of Stats, opens a mode that never asks you anything. The point is meeting the notes you miss again, not generating one more score.

- Pick the notes on a staff map. The ones you miss most are already selected, and weak only · all · clear switch the whole set at once.
- Then you flip through cards with the answers written on them. Nothing is asked and nothing is graded.
- None of it is recorded. Your stats only count what you actually read in a test.

Records are kept per account, so several people on one device each see their own history by switching names.

Records and accounts live on the device. No sign-in, and nothing leaves for a server.
