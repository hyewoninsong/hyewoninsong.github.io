The problem is rarely that you cannot read music. It is that every note starts with counting up from do, and counting never turns into reading no matter how long you do it. You learn the counting path first, then watch the hints come away one step at a time, until a position you used to count is one you read.

## Decide what can come up

Practice goes stale when it starts too wide. Treble and bass clef each turn on and off on their own, and each one you turn on gets its own range.

- **Easy · Medium · Hard** — up to 1, 3, and 5 ledger lines. The ranges stack, so picking Medium keeps the easy notes in the mix.
- The range you pick is drawn on a staff right there, with a count of how many notes it holds, so you know how wide the session is before it starts.
- Turn a clef off and its range is remembered, then restored when you turn it back on. You are not re-picking it every time.

## Five stages, one hint less each time

Handed a bare staff on day one, most people quit within the week. Instead of picking a practice style, you pick where you are on a single ladder.

- **Read do · read do-mi-sol / do-la-fa** — the first two stages show and never ask. There is no note to answer, so there is one Confirm button, no grading and no record. This is where the thirds you step through from do become familiar.
- **do-mi-sol / do-la-fa hint, then answer · do hint, then answer** — the same notes get asked from here, with one hint less each stage: the whole ladder first, then just the do you count from.
- **No hints** — one note on the staff and nothing else. It is the default, and it shows on the spot whether you are ready.
- The counting path is drawn as a grey dotted line **with a note name written at every step**, so you are never guessing where to start or how far to go. The one thing in black ink is the note you have to answer.
- The card sits pinned above Start and begins folded. Folded, it still names the stage you are on.

## There is no finish line

A session is not a fixed number of questions. Notes keep coming until you tap Exit in the top left. Reading with a countdown left in the corner is a different exercise from simply reading.

- A wrong answer never spells the right one out. The note stays on the staff and you read it again. Copying an answer down and moving on is how you miss it the same way next time.
- Each question carries its own clock. Retries after a wrong answer keep that clock running, and only first-try answers count toward your average.
- No progress bar, no remaining count. The screen holds the note you are reading and the answer buttons.
- Exiting leaves you on the Test tab with the settings you just chose, so one more round is one tap away.

## See the weak spots in the shape you read

One accuracy figure does not tell you what to work on. The Stats tab speaks in single notes.

- Positions are coloured on a staff map, so a weak register shows up in the shape you actually read from.
- Tap a position and that note's last five questions and time trend appear right below it — no sheet, no dock.
- The cutoffs between solid and shaky are printed under the map, so you are not reading a shade by guesswork.
- It is built from your last twenty sessions, so something you could not read months ago is not still scoring you.

## One row per session

The Records tab speaks in sessions.

- Date and time, the types you picked, and how many of the questions you got on the first try and how long it took — all on the one row.
- Tap the row and the session opens up: a summary plus a per-question breakdown, so you can see which note ate the time.
- Everything is here. Trimming to the last twenty is the Stats tab's job.

Records are kept per account, so several people on one device each see their own history by switching names.

Records and accounts live on the device. No sign-in, and nothing leaves for a server.
